Appendix · Backtest by year

Backtest by Year

The results appendix shows what the bot earned. This one sits in the chair of a trading desk risk manager and grades it — one year at a time, the way you'd review any trader's book. For each year: did it capture the opportunity the market actually offered? How did it handle its losses and size its risk? Were the right strategies working, or was it winning by luck? A number is only good in the context of the market that produced it — so every year is judged against the gold move it was dealt.

Opportunity captured

Almost none. Gold rose +18.3% — a clean, trend-following year — and the account finished down 2.3%. For a machine built to "buy strength in an uptrend," missing an 18% uptrend is the sharpest indictment on the page. It was still tuning its regime reads early in the sample and never got in gear.

Losses & money management

The saving grace: the losses were disciplined. The worst single trade was −1.1R — no blow-ups, no widened stops, no revenge-sizing. The 11.7% drawdown is the year's real black mark, though: taking a double-digit dip and finishing red is the worst reward-for-risk of the whole run. Capital was preserved, but barely earned its keep.

Strategy fit

Wrong tools for the tape. The short book (−$471, 31% win rate) kept fading a market that was rising — the single worst directional cell of the eight years. Pullback Continuation bled −$483 chasing a trend it couldn't hold. Engulfing (+$443) was the lone bright spot.

D+Verdict: a missed year, redeemed only by discipline. It didn't lose the account, but it left an 18% rally on the table and paid an 11.7% drawdown for the privilege. On a real desk this is the year you have a hard conversation about — not because it lost money, but because it did nothing with an easy market.

C+Verdict: correct instincts, inefficient execution. It read the regime right — shorts and the crash engine leading — but the 16%-dip-for-1%-gain is the year's flaw. Survived a nasty tape leaning the right way; just paid a stiff toll to do it.

This is pure alpha, not captured beta. Buy-and-hold made nothing (−0.3%); the bot made +26.5%. When the return has almost no relationship to the underlying's direction, you're looking at genuine trading skill — extracting money from a 25%-range chop through disciplined two-sided participation.

Ideal balance. Both books profitable — Pin Bar (+$1,277) and Pullback (+$1,031) on the long side, the Crash engine (+$672) and the short book (+$1,767) on the sell side. A−Verdict: the standout "skill" year. +26% from a flat market at a 7.9% drawdown is the single most impressive line on the report card — more so than the bigger-dollar trending years, because none of it was gifted by direction. Just short of an A only because there is always a touch of luck in nailing a two-sided year this cleanly.

The trader's verdict

Eight years reviewed as you'd review any trader's book. A few conclusions a risk desk would actually write down.

It is a genuine two-sided operator, not a closet long-only fund. The tell is 2022 and 2023 — it made 26% and 32% in years gold went nowhere and then only halfway, by trading both sides. Any system can look clever in a bull market; making money from a flat one (2022) is the evidence of real edge rather than borrowed beta.

Its risk management is its best feature — and it is remarkably constant. The worst intra-year dip ranged from 8% to 16% across every regime, and, most tellingly, did not rise in the 76% blow-out year of 2025. A machine that returns 76% at the same drawdown it takes in a break-even year is one that will not blow up chasing a hot streak. That discipline is worth more than any single year's return.

Its defining weakness is the mirror image of that strength: it will not chase. It captured only 15–28% of the peak move available in its trend years and sat out the entire 2019 rally. If you want a machine to wring the last dollar out of a runaway, this is the wrong one. It is built to bank early and sleep well — a deliberate trade of upside for calm.

Two honest risks a desk would flag. First, concentration: 2025 alone is 56% of the whole run's profit, and 2022–2025 is roughly 95% — the long-run record needs trending years to keep arriving. Second, the 2019 and 2026-H1 profile: in its two weakest tapes the raw trade edge thinned to break-even, saved by discipline and diversification rather than signal quality. The machine survives bad years; it does not yet profit from them.

Overall grade: a solid A−. As a risk-adjusted vehicle it is excellent — it beat gold over the full run at half the drawdown, made money in flat years, and never lost its head in the big one. It is marked down from an A only for the 2019 miss and its structural dependence on trending years. If this were a trader on the desk, you would keep them, size them confidently, and simply not expect miracles in the flat, rangebound years — because on the evidence, that is exactly when they merely tread water.
