The Heartbeat

Everything the bot does hangs off a single clock: the 1-hour candle. Understand the heartbeat and the rest of the book falls into place — because every other chapter is just one beat of this loop, examined slowly.

Once an hour, on purpose

The bot makes exactly one new trade decision when each hourly candle finishes — and only ever acts on a finished hour.

Picture a careful shopper who checks the shelf at the top of each hour instead of fidgeting over every price tag change. When the hour closes, the bot wakes, runs its whole routine start to finish, then goes quiet until the next hour.

Why the hour, and not the minute or the tick? Because acting slowly is a feature, not a limitation. Markets are noisy: prices jump around constantly, and most of that movement is meaningless wiggle that reverses within minutes. A bot that reacted to every flicker would be whipsawed — buying a spike that immediately fades, selling a dip that instantly recovers. By waiting for a full hour of price action to resolve into one finished candle, the bot filters out the noise and only responds to something that actually held for an hour.

This one choice — the hourly heartbeat — is the root of the bot's whole personality: patient, deliberate, and hard to fake out. Every strategy, filter and exit you'll meet in later chapters is timed to it.

Closed candles, never forming

A candle is closed once its hour is over — its four prices can never change again. A forming candle is the current, still-moving one. The bot builds its decisions almost entirely on closed candles.

Every candle records four prices for its slice of time: where it opened, the high it reached, the low, and where it closed. While the hour is still running, all four of those (except the open) are still moving — the "high so far" can be beaten a second later, the close isn't decided until the very end. Acting on that half-formed picture is dangerous, because it can completely reverse before the hour finishes. Traders call that being "repainted."

So the bot is deliberately conservative. Its trend reading, its market-mood label, its tide line, its structure checks, its trailing stop, and its exit signals all read the last completed candle — the one that's now fixed history.

The deliberate exceptions. A few checks read the live, still-moving price on purpose — to "front-run" a fast reversal against a stable, closed-bar baseline. The macro and crash-fade price checks use the live price by design, and the execution step naturally uses the live price and spread the instant it places an order. These are conscious trade-offs, not oversights.

The practical upshot: when this book says a decision is made "on the hourly chart," it almost always means on the candle that just finished — not the one still in progress.

The hourly loop

When the hour closes, the bot runs the same seven-step routine every single time. Those seven steps are the chapters of this book.

Here is the whole loop at a glance. Each step links to the chapter that takes it apart:

Read the room

Build a four-part picture of the market — the favoured direction, the mood, the macro backdrop, and the session — before looking at any specific trade.

Interview the scouts

Ask a panel of specialist strategies whether each sees its particular setup right now.

Pick the single best

Score every "yes" and hire exactly one idea — the strongest this hour. Everyone else is dropped until next hour.

Run the gauntlet

Put that one winner through a wall of twelve safety checks. A single failure cancels the trade.

Size to the risk

Work backwards from the stop-loss so that being wrong costs a fixed, small slice of the account.

Enter

Place the order, re-checking the live price and spread one final time.

Manage & exit

Bank profit in slices, trail a runner up, and close the trade on the exit rules.

Decide once, manage always

There's one important asymmetry: new trades are decided only once an hour, but open trades are managed continuously — on every price tick.

The hourly heartbeat governs opening. But once a trade is live, the bot can't afford to look away for an hour at a time — a stop-loss or a profit target could be reached at any second. So the management side runs constantly in the background: the moment price touches a take-profit level, a stop, or a trailing-stop line, the bot acts, whatever the clock says.

Think of it as two rhythms overlaid: a slow, deliberate once-an-hour pulse for finding and starting trades, and a fast, ever-watchful tick-by-tick pulse for protecting and harvesting the trades already on. Chapter 6 covers that management side in full.

Seven timeframes at once

The hourly candle is the heartbeat, but it's not the only chart the bot watches. A good human trader reads several timeframes together — and so does this bot.

A timeframe is simply how much time each candle covers. The bot reads seven of them, each doing a specific job, all feeding the one hourly decision:

TimeframeWhat each candle coversWhat the bot uses it for
W1 weeklyone weekThe biggest-picture tide — the weekly average's slope, and last week's high/low as major levels
D1 dailyone dayThe long-term trend, the "death cross," and yesterday's high/low
H4 4-hourfour hoursThe market mood (trend vs chop vs volatile) and the trend gate most trades must agree with
H1 hourlyone hourThe heartbeat — one decision per finished candle; most triggers, the tide line, the trailing stop
M15 15-minfifteen minutesThe overnight Asian range and the trigger candles for a few precise strategies
M5 5-minfive minutesOne input to the abnormal-shock detector (a fast-spike check)
live tickright nowThe instant price and spread used at the moment of decision and execution

The mental model is a nested one: the weekly sets the tide, the daily the trend, the 4-hour the mood, the hourly the heartbeat, and the faster charts the fine detail. Everything bigger and everything smaller is read in support of that one hourly decision. The next chapter — Reading the Room — shows exactly how those layers combine into the bot's first move each hour.
