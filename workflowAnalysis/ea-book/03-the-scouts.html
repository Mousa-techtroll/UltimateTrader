The Scouts

Picture the bot as a hiring manager who, once an hour, interviews a panel of specialist scouts. Each is an expert in exactly one chart shape. The manager asks them all "do you see your setup?", scores every yes, and hires the single best. This chapter meets every scout — what it looks for, how it works, and why it's a sound idea.

How the panel works

Twelve scouts sit on the panel, grouped into four families. They don't all fire independently — they compete, and only one is hired per hour.

Each hour, every scout examines the just-closed candle and either stays silent or raises its hand with a setup. Each "yes" is scored on how well it fits the room from the previous chapter — trend agreement, market mood, macro tilt, pattern strength — and graded. The highest-scoring idea is hired; the rest are dropped until next hour. Chapter 4 covers that scoring and selection in detail; here we focus on the scouts themselves.

Two labels appear on every card below. Direction tells you whether a scout can bet up (long), down (short), or both. Status tells you what it actually does in practice — because, as you'll see, being on the panel and actually playing are two different things.

Candle-shape scouts

4 scouts · the workhorses

The simplest and most reliable readers: they judge the market from the shape of the just-finished hourly candle, always in agreement with the bigger 4-hour trend. Three of these do the overwhelming majority of the bot's actual trading.

Pin Bar

Active

A candle with a long tail and a small body — price stabbed one way and got slapped straight back.

During the hour, price pushed hard in one direction, then reversed and closed near where it started, leaving a long "rejection wick". The bot buys when the tail points down (sellers were rejected) inside an uptrend, and sells the mirror image — but never during New York, where short pin bars historically misfire.

Why it works: a long rejection wick marks the exact spot where a push ran out of buyers or sellers. Entering as the reversal confirms lets the bot ride the bounce with a tight, logical stop just past the rejected extreme.

Engulfing

Active

A single up-candle that completely swallows the previous down-candle — buyers overpowering sellers.

In one hour, buyers fully reversed and overwhelmed the prior hour's sellers: the new candle's body wraps the whole of the last one. A clean flip of control. It only fires when the 4-hour trend already agrees, so it's catching continuation, not fighting the tide. Long only. The v1 release adds two quality guards — it won't buy into a daily "death cross", and it drops the mis-graded middle "A"-grade setups that used to lose — which lifted it from +$8,100 to +$10,624 on fewer, cleaner trades.

Why it works: a full engulf is one of the least ambiguous signs that control has changed hands. Pairing it with the higher-timeframe uptrend filters out random noise and keeps the bot buying strength.

MA Cross

Active

A fast 10-hour average crossing up through a slow 21-hour average — "momentum just turned up."

The classic trend trigger: the short-term average lifting through the longer-term one signals recent momentum has flipped upward. It fires only in a clearly trending market and is blocked during New York. Long only — the downward-cross version was measured to lose money and removed from the code.

Why it works: fewer but stronger signals. It fires several times less often than the other candle scouts, yet each trade carries more follow-through — the payoff of waiting for a cleaner, harder-to-fake trigger.

Displacement

Dormant

A stop-hunt followed by a big, decisive drive the other way.

Price first dips below a recent low to trip other traders' stop orders, then a large "power candle" closes back above it — the signature of big money grabbing cheap liquidity and then revealing its true direction. The bot enters after the trap, using the failed level as its stop.

Why it's a good idea: trading after the trap, not into it, catches the real move with a clean invalidation point. In practice an hourly candle that large right after a sweep is so rare on gold that this scout almost never fires.

Range scouts

2 scouts · the sharpshooters

These read a faster 15-minute chart to time reversals at the edges of a range, then size the trade from the 1-hour volatility. They fire rarely, but when they do their setups are among the highest-quality in the book.

Range-Edge Fade (S3)

Dormant

A false breakout at the edge of a box — price pokes out, then snaps back in.

When gold is boxed inside a range, price often pokes just past the floor or ceiling to trigger breakout traders, then fails and snaps back inside. On the 15-minute chart, this scout fades that failure back toward the middle of the box, targeting the opposite edge.

Why it's a good idea: most range breakouts fail. Fading the failure at the very edge gives a naturally good reward-to-risk. But its full checklist has to line up perfectly, which has never once happened on gold in the test window.

Failed-Break Reversal (S6)

Active

A failed stop-hunt at a key level — the breakout was a trap.

Price spikes past an important level (like yesterday's high or low) to grab a cluster of orders, then reclaims the level on the 15-minute chart. The breakout that fooled everyone becomes the bot's entry, with a stop just past the failed spike.

Why it works: reclaiming a swept level is one of the cleanest reversals in trading. This scout is tiny — a couple of times a year — but its trades are the sharpest in the whole book. Its short side is switched off (it was measured to lose).

Breakout scouts

2 scouts · the specialists

These wait for price to break out of its recent range with force. One is a pure-trend breakout kept for volatile markets; the other is the bot's only dedicated bear-market engine.

Volatility Breakout

Dormant

A clean punch out of the recent 20-bar range while the trend agrees.

Price closes beyond both its recent high and a volatility band, during a genuinely volatile market, with a strong trend reading and the 4-hour averages stacked in agreement. A high bar to clear on purpose.

Why it's a good idea: real range-expansion moves are worth catching. But it's deliberately gated to only volatile regimes, making it a rare specialist — effectively a reserve that almost never activates.

Crash Breakout (Rubber Band)

Active

A fade of an over-stretched bounce inside a confirmed downtrend.

This is the one scout built for falling markets. It only wakes when the daily 50-day average is below the 200-day average — a confirmed "death cross" downtrend, and in the v1 release only a fresh one (within a few months of the cross), so it never fires on a stale, played-out downtrend. Then, when live price rubber-bands more than two volatility-units above its hourly mean, it shorts the snap-back toward that mean, at any hour of the day and only on top-grade setups.

Why it's a good idea: in a real downtrend, sharp bounces are exhaustion, and selling them back to the mean is a textbook fade. It's the bot's only participant when gold is falling — valuable diversification even though its wins are modest.

Multi-mode engines

4 engines · the sophisticates

Each of these is really several sub-strategies bundled together, reporting the first that fires. They encode the more advanced "smart-money", session and continuation ideas — though most of their conditions turn out too demanding to fire on gold.

Liquidity engine

Dormant

A panel of four "smart-money" reads, reporting the first that fires.

It checks, in priority order: a Displacement move, an Order-Block Retest (price returning to a zone a big institutional candle left behind), a Fair-Value-Gap fill, and a Swing-Failure. The idea is to trade where large orders have left footprints on the chart.

Why it's a good idea: big orders really do leave repeatable footprints — zones, gaps and sweeps. This is the sophisticated end of the book. In practice its structural conditions never all lined up on gold's hourly chart in the test window.

Session engine

Off

Time-of-day breakout modes — London open, New York continuation, and more.

Four session-timed strategies, each firing only inside its own window off the overnight Asian range. All four ship switched off — they measured a 0% win rate in testing. The engine stays loaded only because it also runs the bot's master clock and builds the Asian range every other scout relies on. One caveat worth knowing: session-breakout logic isn't entirely absent from the live book — the Expansion engine below quietly runs a copy of it, which does trade. So it's this engine and its four modes that are off, not the idea itself.

Why it's here: session edges are real for some instruments and worth having on the shelf. On gold they didn't pay, so they're disabled — but the engine remains as essential timekeeping infrastructure.

Expansion engine

Active

Energy building then releasing — a coil, then a break.

It looks for an oversized "institutional" candle that closes near its extreme, waits for price to coil quietly for a few bars, then enters on the break of that big candle's extreme. Compression, then expansion. It also carries a second engine inside it: a session breakout — the very London/Asian-range logic the Session engine above ships switched off. On the backtest record that composed sub-strategy accounts for a large share of Expansion's trades (roughly half), all re-tagged under the "Expansion" name — which is why the roster can look like it has no live session breakout when it quietly does.

Why it works: quiet-then-explosive is a genuine, repeatable market rhythm. This is the bot's only live breakout expression and its cleanest true diversifier — small, two-sided, and it earns its keep in high-volatility stretches.

Pullback Continuation

Active

Buying the dip inside an uptrend (or selling the bounce in a downtrend).

After an established trend pauses for a shallow pullback that shows signs of exhaustion, a "reclaim" candle closes back over the recent highs and the bot joins the trend at a better price. The textbook continuation trade.

Why it's a good idea: joining a strong trend on a pullback gives a favourable entry with the trend as a tailwind. Honest note: this used to be the weakest live scout, giving its profit back — until the v1 release removed its mis-graded "A"-grade cohort (the specific setups that lost). That one gate lifted its profit factor from a barely-there 1.12 to 2.04.

The honest reality

Twelve strategies are loaded, but that's a fact about the roster, not the playing time.

Seven really play, and three carry the game

Over 7.5 years of real gold data, five of the twelve produced zero trades — their internal conditions are simply too demanding to fire on this market, even with their switches on (use the "Dormant" and "Switched off" filters above to see which). The live book is really about seven scouts, and three of them — Pin Bar, Engulfing and MA Cross — do the overwhelming majority of the work.

That isn't a flaw so much as honesty about what actually earns: the bot is, at heart, a disciplined way to buy bullish hourly momentum candles when gold is trending up, wrapped in a great deal of machinery that keeps it out of trouble the rest of the time. The dormant scouts aren't dead weight — they're a reserve that would need re-tuning, not just switching on, to ever contribute.
