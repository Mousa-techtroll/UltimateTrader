Start to Finish

Every chapter so far examined one link of the chain in slow motion. This closing chapter runs the whole thing at speed on a single illustrative trade — then leaves you with a reference desk: which timeframe drives each decision, and a plain-English glossary of every term used in the book.

One trade, start to finish

The whole chain on one trade. The numbers are invented for clarity and imply no outcome — the point is to watch every link connect.

The hour closes.

The bot wakes and reads the room: daily, 4-hour and hourly trends all up; market mood trending; a calm New-York session. Buying is firmly favoured. (Chapter 2.)

Hourly heartbeat

A scout raises its hand.

A bullish engulfing candle just closed near $2,400.00. The pattern's low — the point that would prove it wrong — sits at $2,396.00. It wins the hour's interview. (Chapter 3.)

Engulfing · H1 trigger + H4 gate

It runs the gauntlet.

Kill switch off; not halted; no price shock; session quality fine; spread normal; not flip-flopping; a solid quality grade; one of five slots used. Every gate says yes. (Chapter 4.)

12 gates

Stop & reward are set.

The stop goes just below the pattern low, about $2,395.50 — a stop distance of roughly $4.50. The laddered targets clear the "reward ≥ 1.3× risk" check.

R-geometry

Size is worked backwards.

It risks about 1% — say $100. With a $4.50 stop, that's roughly $100 ÷ $4.50 ≈ 22 ounces (about 0.22 of a lot), rounded down to the broker's step. (Chapter 5.)

Pure arithmetic

The order goes in.

The bot re-checks the live price and spread one final time, then places the buy.

Live execution

It manages to the exit.

A 15% nibble at +0.7R; the stop slides to break-even at +0.8R; 40% banked at +1.3R, 30% at +1.8R; the runner rides the trailing stop until price taps it — or a housekeeping exit fires first. (Chapter 6.)

Continuous management

Illustrative only. Prices, sizes and steps are invented to show the mechanism; no profit, win rate or outcome is implied. The bot makes no promises about any individual trade — only that every trade follows this same disciplined chain, in this same order, every hour.

Glossary

Every term used in this book, in plain language.

Candle / bar
One block summarizing a slice of time by four prices: where it opened, its high, its low, and where it closed. "Closed" = finished and fixed; "forming" = still moving.
Timeframe
How much time each candle covers — M5 (5-min), M15 (15-min), H1 (hourly), H4 (4-hour), D1 (daily), W1 (weekly).
Long / short
Long = betting price goes up (buying). Short = betting price goes down (selling).
Trend / bias
The trend is where price is heading; the bot's bias is the direction it's predisposed to trade — heavily tilted toward buying gold.
Regime
The type of market right now: trending, ranging, choppy, or volatile — decided on the 4-hour chart.
Session
A regional trading window — Asia, London, New York. A clock concept, not a chart.
Spread
The small gap between the buy and sell price — a built-in cost every time you enter.
ATR
Average True Range — how much price typically moves per bar lately. In short: "how bouncy is it?"
ADX
A gauge of how strong a trend is (in any direction). High = a strong march; low = aimless drifting.
EMA / moving average
A smoothed average price line. The 200-period average is a long-term "tide line" for the big picture.
Bollinger Bands
An envelope drawn around price that widens when it's volatile and narrows when calm.
Stop-loss
The pre-set "I was wrong, get me out" exit that caps a loss. This bot's stop only ever tightens.
Take-profit
A pre-set price where the bot cashes in some or all of a winning trade.
R / R-multiple
"R" is the amount risked on a trade. Targets and break-even are measured in R — a 1.3R target is a gain of 1.3× the risk.
Lot / size
The trade-size unit. A standard gold lot is 100 ounces; the bot trades fractions, sized to the risk.
Drawdown
How far the account has fallen from its recent peak. The bot brakes its risk as drawdown grows.
Liquidity / stop-hunt
Liquidity is how easily you can trade without moving price. A "stop-hunt" is a poke past a level to trigger others' stops before reversing.
Death cross
When a shorter average (50-day) falls below a longer one (200-day) — a classic confirmed-downtrend signal.
Smart-money (SMC)
Reading the chart around where large institutional orders likely sit — order blocks, gaps, sweeps. Used here mainly as a quality check.
Weekend gap
The jump between Friday's close and Sunday's reopen; the bot flattens on Friday to avoid it.
