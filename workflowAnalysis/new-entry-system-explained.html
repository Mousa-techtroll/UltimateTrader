System Design — Illustrated

One substrate, four strategies, a regime that chooses.

How the redesigned UltimateTrader entry system works: a regime router that activates and weights four distinct major-strategy engines — Trend-Continuation, Reversal, Range, Expansion — all sharing one top-down confluence substrate and one IMarketContext. Built to be read by a trader and a developer alike.

00 /  The question

"Is this all one strategy in one direction?" — Yes. That's the flaw.

The first redesign produced a single, largely long-continuation confluence engine. It is far better than the old bag of triggers — but the user is right to push: a system that only knows one thesis (buy strength in discount) is still a concentration bet. When gold ranges or rolls over, a one-thesis engine has nothing to switch to. The answer is to host multiple major strategies, each with its own market thesis, regime, and direction, coordinated by a router.

The key design move that makes this safe rather than chaotic: the four engines do not each reinvent analysis. They share one set of IMarketContext primitives and one orthogonal-axis confluence score. What differs between them is the thesis — which liquidity event they hunt, which regime they trust, and which direction they're allowed to take. This is the cure for the long-concentration risk: the system becomes genuinely multi-directional and multi-regime, without becoming thirteen uncorrelated codebases.

The verdict on single vs multi

Multi-strategy — four engines on one substrate, with a regime router. One engine cannot express both "buy the discount pullback in a trend" and "fade the swept extreme in a range" without becoming a tangle of special-cases. Separate engines keep each thesis clean and independently measurable, while the shared substrate prevents drift and double-counting. A single engine wins only if you believe gold is always trending — and the disabled graveyard plus the 2021/2022–23 ranges say it isn't.

01 /  The shared substrate

Every engine walks the same five layers, top-down

Whatever the thesis, a trade is built from the top of the chart down. The HTF establishes where and which direction matters; the LTF supplies the trigger. The engines differ only in which layer carries their edge — a Trend engine leans on the draw and the discount zone; a Reversal engine lives or dies on the sweep + structure shift. The layers are the common language.

02 /  The regime router

The router decides which thesis is allowed to speak

The router is the new top-level coordinator. Each bar it reads the regime from IMarketContext — trend direction, ADX, ATR/volatility regime, Choppiness Index, day-type, macro/DXY bias, and the crash/death-cross flags — and activates and weights the engines whose thesis fits the current market. A Range engine should be silent in a strong trend; a Reversal engine should be loud after a death-cross. The router never invents signals; it gates and weights the four engines, then the best-scoring surviving signal trades (one per bar, as today).

"Weight" maps directly onto the existing risk plumbing: the router writes the engine's activation into regime_risk_multiplier / session_risk_multiplier on the EntrySignal, so a half-weighted engine in a marginal regime simply trades smaller — it doesn't need a separate kill switch. This is the concentration cure made concrete: on a bull-trend day the book leans long via engine ①+④; on a balance day it can be short via ③; after a death-cross it can be aggressively counter-trend via ②. The system is no longer one direction.

03 /  The four major strategies

Distinct theses, one substrate

Each engine is a CEntryStrategy-derived major strategy with its own market thesis, permitted direction(s), regime window, and confluence recipe — but all consume the same IMarketContext and emit the same EntrySignal with engine_mode, qualityScore, engine_confluence and day_type set. Where their edge concentrates on the five-layer stack differs, and that difference is the whole point.

Why these four and not thirteen

Thirteen plugins collapse into four theses because most plugins are restatements of one of four market mechanics: continuation, reversal-at-liquidity, mean-reversion-in-balance, expansion-from-compression. Grouping by thesis is what finally makes engines independent — engine ① and engine ③ make opposite bets in opposite regimes, so they genuinely diversify instead of stacking the same long-momentum trade seven ways (the original flaw).

04 /  Re-judging the graveyard

Reactivate, reimplement, or retire — on concept + impl reality

The forensic premise is that bad implementation or unfair gating — not weak concept — may have buried these. That's partly true and I verified the worst offender directly: in CSetupEvaluator.mqh the Factor-4 pattern-quality ladder matches by comment token, and several plugins emit a string that matches no token, so they silently earn 0 of 2 points and get starved to a low tier. Confirmed: the plugin emits "Bullish Liquidity Sweep" but the token is "LiquiditySweep" (no space); "Support Bounce" and "False Breakout Fade" have no Factor-4 token at all. That is a real, cheap-to-fix starvation — and it means their negative numbers were never a fair test of the concept.

But starvation explains a low tier; it does not make a counter-trend concept good on a bull metal. Where mt5-developer reports CLEAN-IMPL, I have to say plainly: the concept, not the code, is the problem. Each verdict below is justified from market structure first, impl reality second, and routed to one of the four engines if it lives.

The discipline that prevents re-folklore

Notice the pattern: everything REACTIVATEd or REIMPLEMENTed has a structural reason to work on gold (sweep-reclaim, balance rotation, squeeze-expansion, trend pullback) and a verified impl issue. Everything RETIREd is either a clock-based folklore (London/NY/Silver Bullet), a fragile conjunction (Panic), or a counter-trend trigger with no anchor (Bearish Engulf/MA-Cross) — and the CLEAN-IMPL ones prove the point: when the code is fine and it still has no edge, the concept was the problem. We are not keeping a dead idea just because a comment-string fix would let it fire.

05 /  The orthogonal-axis score

Location and liquidity own the points; momentum is capped

All four engines write into the same qualityScore (0–10) across orthogonal axes, so two signals on the same axis count once. This is what stops the system double-counting "buy strength." The visual below shows the budget: 7 of 10 points are where and why (location + liquidity + structure); momentum/timing is deliberately starved to 3.

06 /  Worked setups, drawn

What each engine actually fires

① Trend-Continuation — A long (the system's bread and butter)

Thesis
H4 uptrend; draw is the next buy-side pool above. Resting state = hunt a discount pullback.
Spine (L3)
M15 sweeps the inducement low below an H1 bullish OB and reclaims; M5 displacement up.
Entry (L4)
OB mitigation / OTE 0.705 of the displacement leg, in discount. engine_mode=MODE_OB_RETEST. Absorbs the rescued Pullback-Continuation (after the line-747 ATR fix) and displacement-origin FVG re-entry (rescued FVG-Mitigation).
Stop / Targets
SL below OB origin; TP1 equilibrium, TP2 prior swing high, TP3 upside draw.
Score
draw 2 + discount 2 + zone 2 + sweep 1 = 7 → A (1.0%); +confirm/flow → A+.

④ Breakout / Expansion — trend-aligned long out of a coil

Thesis
ATR was compressed (squeeze); the router sees expansion beginning, H4 trend up.
Spine (L3)
A ≥1.8×ATR displacement candle breaks the consolidation high after a minor sweep of the coil's internal low (so it's initiative, not a stop-run into the break).
Entry (L4)
Prefer the retest of the broken consolidation edge / the FVG the breakout left — better R than chasing the break. Absorbs Vol-Breakout, IC Breakout, the rescued Compression BO.
Flow (L5)
Tick-volume expansion on the breakout bar (proxy only on spot). No real CVD/DOM on XAUUSD.
Score
draw 2 + zone 2 (retest) + sweep 1 + flow 1 = 6 → A; breakout-chase entry without retest caps lower.

07 /  For the developer

How this maps onto the plugin architecture

The architecture already carries most of what's needed. IMarketContext exposes regime, H4 trend, ADX/ATR, volatility regime, Choppiness, BOS, OB/FVG state, SMC confluence, macro/DXY and the crash/death-cross flags. The build is a re-wiring around a new router, not a new analytics stack.

  • Regime Router — a new top-level coordinator (sits where signal collection happens, alongside CSignalOrchestrator). Reads IMarketContext, sets each engine's activation/weight, writes weight into regime_risk_multiplier. It does not generate signals.
  • Four engine plugins — CTrendContinuationEngine, CReversalSweepEngine, CRangeReversionEngine, CExpansionEngine (the last already exists and is extended), each : public CEntryStrategy, each setting engine_mode on its EntrySignal. They reuse CLiquidityEngine sweep/OB detection rather than reimplementing it.
  • Demoted triggers — CEngulfingEntry / CPinBarEntry become L5 confirmation helpers the engines query; they no longer fire standalone.
  • The Factor-4 fix is mandatory and cheap — in CSetupEvaluator.mqh: rename plugin comments (or the tokens) so "Bullish Liquidity Sweep"→"LiquiditySweep" matches, and add tokens for "Support Bounce" and "False Breakout". Until this is done, several rescued strategies stay starved no matter how good the concept. Verified at CSetupEvaluator.mqh:196–229.
  • Repaint fixes before any re-enable backtest — shift-0 → shift-1 indicator reads in BB-MR, Range Box, False-Break Fade (RSI is its trigger), and Pullback-Continuation line 747. A backtest on repainting logic is not a valid test of the concept.
  • Scoring lives in the evaluator — the orthogonal axes are computed in EvaluateSetupQuality (the real gate), not in the plugins (whose qualityScore is overwritten). Keep m_smc_min_confluence (default 60) on engine_confluence as the independent SMC-structure check.
  • Route CFileEntry through the same evaluator so external signals are tiered, not bypassed.
Validation plan — so it's falsifiable, not just prettier

XAUUSD+ H1, full 2019–2026, A/B vs the current ensemble. Fix the Factor-4 tokens and repaint reads first (otherwise the re-enable tests are invalid). Expect fewer trades, higher avg-R, and — the real test — positive contribution from engines ②/③ during the 2021 and 2022–23 gold ranges/pullbacks, where a one-direction system bleeds. Log per-engine and per-axis attribution. Kill criteria: drop any engine with avg-R ≤ 0 over the sample; drop the killzone-timing axis if its attribution is non-positive (don't keep it out of ICT loyalty); revert to the single-engine redesign if the multi-engine version doesn't beat it net of the added complexity.
