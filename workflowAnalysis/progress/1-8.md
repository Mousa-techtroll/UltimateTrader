  **Q1 — Is byte-identity the correct/expected outcome (degenerate-forming-bar mechanism sound)? YES.**
  Verified from source: `CMarketContext::Update()` early-returns unless a new H1 bar formed (271–274), so it runs exactly once per H1 bar, at the new-bar tick — the instant the cached values are consumed. At that instant the forming bar `[0]` has just opened → `high[0]≈low[0]≈open[0]` (degenerate, ~zero range). `m_swing_high/low` (818–831, confirmed) are a `MathMax`/`MathMin` over a `m_swing_lookback`-bar window; production `InpSwingLookback=20` (Inputs:99, passed unchanged at mq5:499). For the cached swing to move, the degenerate forming-bar open would have to be the single highest high (or lowest low) of the 20-bar window — a fresh bar's open is essentially never the 20-bar extreme. So the old `[0..19]` and new `[1..20]` windows return identical extremes at the decision point. MA200 is even stronger: `[1]` is a fully-settled value, identical whether copied as 1 or 2 bars. The swing shift therefore *genuinely cannot* move the cached extremes at the once-per-bar consumption point → byte-identical across 2024/2023/2020 (md5-matching, cmp clean) is the necessary, expected result. This is the **1.5/1.6 settled-value class** (look-ahead that can't bite at the decision point), NOT the **1.2–1.4 indicator-deflation class** (forming-bar ADX/ATR/MA-close that genuinely differ at bar-open). The edit still correctly removes the intrabar repaint in the cached values; it simply has no steady-state effect on this config — which the three stress-diverse years (trend / mixed / COVID-gap) confirm.
