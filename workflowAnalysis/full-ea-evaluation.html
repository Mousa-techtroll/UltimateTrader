01 /  Executive summary

What the audit found, and how much to trust it

The EA is broadly Adequate with a load-bearing core that is correct — but several subsystems are Weak, and the most serious issues cluster in three places: SMC / structure detection, the confluence-scoring pipeline, and the new, un-backtested multi-strategy engines. Nothing flagged here is a crash-on-the-default-path; the danger is in mis-scored trades, silently disabled safety gates, and a dormant engine set built to re-enable strategies the repo's own forensics already killed.

Why 48 disputed is a feature, not noise

The 48 disputed findings are a trust signal. Every one was raised by a first-pass reviewer and then rejected by an adversarial second pass that checked it against the actual source — usually because the cited code is dead, double-gated off by default, self-healing after warmup, or mathematically unreachable on the live XAUUSD path. A review that confirmed everything it raised would be a review that wasn't really checking. The 71 confirmed + 13 needs-fix items below are what is left after that filter, which is why they deserve attention.

The honest read: the live, default-configuration EA is a long-biased gold system whose baseline risk math, execution retcode handling, persistence (CRC-verified, POD-only), and trailing ratchet are sound. The confirmed defects bite along three seams — (1) market-structure reads taken from the still-forming bar instead of the last closed bar, recurring across SMC, regime, trailing and file-signal code; (2) a confluence-scoring redesign that is computed and then discarded, with a hard-gate “spine” that never resets; and (3) portfolio- and multi-symbol-level risk controls (aggregate-exposure cap, symbol isolation, daily-trade-count) that are documented but not actually enforced. The single critical finding is forward-looking: the multi-strategy scaffold exists to flip on engines that recompose measured losers, and the dead-path defense lives in config flags, not in the engines themselves.

02 /  Architecture map

How a tick becomes a managed position

The EA is a layered pipeline. Market analysis builds a shared IMarketContext once per new H1 bar; entry plugins and the four multi-strategy engines read that context to emit candidate EntrySignals; a validation/scoring stage assigns a quality tier and risk %; the orchestrator ranks one signal per bar and hands it to the executor; and the position coordinator manages trailing, partial TPs and exits, persisting state to a CRC-verified file. The recurring fault line, marked below, is that several producers read the forming bar [0] where the convention is the closed bar [1].

Trading logic — signals & edge

Trade-Signal
Weak
7 open/ 8 reviewed 1 crit 3 high

The feat/multi-strategy branch layers a four-engine + regime-router + 'orthogonal' confluence scorer on top of the v18 long-biased gold EA, gated OFF by default (InpEnableMultiStrategy=false), so nothing here is harming the live baseline today. But as a design it does not serve a real gold edge: the engines re-compose and re-activate strategies the repo's own forensics killed (FVG Mitigation PF 0.61, Pullback -0.5R, BB Mean Reversion -1.1R, SFP/Liquidity-Sweep 0% WR, short-side reversal logic), and the new scorer that was supposed to discipline them is computed and then discarded — the legacy comment-token evaluator overwrites setupQuality/qualityScore, while the scorer's 'hard gate' rides a latched never-reset BOS flag and its axes are not orthogonal (dealing-range, equilibrium, premium/discount all derive from one swing pair, double-counting confluence). Net: the multi-strategy thesis is incoherent against the measured edge and must not be enabled until each composed path independently beats the v18 baseline with a stated kill-criterion.

MarketAnalysis — SMC / order-blocks / FVG / liquidity

MA-SMC
Weak
3 open/ 7 reviewed 2 high

The SMC engine detects order blocks and FVGs on closed bars correctly (OB scan starts at i+1>=4, FVG at i>=1, geometry is sound) and manages indicator handles cleanly (single ATR handle created in Init, INVALID_HANDLE checked, released in destructor). However two materially-wrong behaviors undermine the liquidity and structure components that feed live entry confluence: liquidity pools are scanned only once at Init and never refreshed (so they go stale immediately and the "recent sweep" flag is a permanent latch), and BOS/CHoCH/sweep detection reads the forming bar [0] inside a once-per-new-bar update window so real breaks are routinely missed or read inconsistently. CHoCH logic is also geometrically incomplete. These flow into CLiquidityEngine, CTrendContinuationEngine, CConfluenceScorer and CSignalValidator, so the defects affect real trade scoring.

Infrastructure

Infra
Weak
3 open/ 9 reviewed 1 high

Only two of these eleven files are actually wired into the production EA: the global Logger (`Log`) and `CErrorHandler` (plus `ErrorHandlingUtils`, which the trade executor calls on every failed send). The remaining health/recovery/timeout/concurrency stack — including the entire `CComponentManager` and `CEnhancedPositionManager` that own them — is included (so it compiles) but never instantiated or invoked, making most of this subsystem dead code that nonetheless carries real bugs (inverted health comparisons, a memory leak, and a recovery routine that fires a live market order). The code is heavily defensive and verbose but the abstractions are largely unused and the health-gating it advertises does not run.

MarketAnalysis — regime / trend / macro

MA-Regime
Weak
7 open/ 11 reviewed

The regime/trend/macro detectors are functionally wired and correctly create handles once in Init() with INVALID_HANDLE checks, but they share a systemic repaint/look-ahead defect: CTrendDetector, CRegimeClassifier, CMacroBias, CMomentumFilter and CMarketContext all read indicator buffers and price at shift [0] (the forming bar) even though CMarketContext.Update() is gated to fire exactly at the open of a new H1 bar — so trend/regime/macro classifications are derived from a bar that has essentially no data yet, and CCrashDetector is internally inconsistent (D1 EMAs at [0] vs close at [1]). CRangeBoxDetector, by contrast, correctly uses closed bars ([1]). Secondary issues include an unguarded IndicatorRelease in CRegimeClassifier's destructor with handles never initialized in its constructor, a price-scale-dependent MACD score that saturates on gold, and several dead/tautological code paths. None cause an outright crash in the normal path, but the shift-0 reads materially distort the market-state signals that gate every entry.

Cross-cutting — data / persistence / pipeline / performance

XCut
Adequate
6 open/ 8 reviewed 2 high

The end-to-end signal→validation→ranking→execution→management→persistence pipeline is well-engineered: state saves are event-driven (not per-tick), the binary state file is CRC32-verified over POD-only structs, the executor robustly handles netting-ticket correction and instant-TP-closed cases, confirmation checks read the closed bar, and lot sizing guards division-by-zero. Main EA + Inputs

TopLevel
Adequate
7 open/ 8 reviewed 1 high

The top-level wiring is structurally sound: new-bar gating is correct (capture-then-update, act on closed bar [1]), every `new` in OnInit has matching teardown in reverse order in OnDeinit, indicator handles in the only local handle-creating helper (ShouldBlockLongExtensionCore) are short-read-guarded and released, and the multi-strategy additions are cleanly gated behind a default-off master flag. Core-Orch
Adequate

The orchestration core is structurally sound: confirmation logic reads the closed bar (rates[1]) with no look-ahead, the signal ranking is one-per-bar, positions are removed in reverse order, CAdaptiveTPManager creates/checks/releases handles correctly, and state persistence is CRC-verified. Trading logic — risk / exits / regime

Trade-Risk
Adequate
6 open/ 7 reviewed 1 high

The risk and exit architecture is coherent and respects the repo's measured long-biased gold edge: position sizing is correct fixed-fractional math with per-point value derived properly, the EC v3 controller and regime scaler are independently bounded with floors, and the "trailing is sacred" Chandelier exit is left intact per the empirical Core Truths. None crash or place mathematically wrong single trades, but the portfolio-level and accounting issues materially weaken drawdown control.

Execution + Risk

Exec-Risk
Adequate
5 open/ 8 reviewed 1 high

The risk plugins (CQualityTierRiskStrategy, CATRBasedRiskStrategy) are clean: correct money-based sizing math, proper lot normalization/clamping, ATR handle created once in Initialize() with INVALID_HANDLE checks and released in Deinitialize(), and short-read guards on CopyBuffer. Multi-strategy scaffold — 4 engines + base + scorer + router

Engines-Multi
Adequate
7 open/ 8 reviewed

The MQL5 mechanics are clean and correct: indicator handles are created once in Initialize() with INVALID_HANDLE checks and released in Deinitialize(), every ATR/BB/RSI read uses the CLOSED bar [1] with proper ArraySetAsSeries and CopyBuffer/Copy* short-read guards, all enums/struct fields/IMarketContext methods used actually exist, and OnInit wiring (SetContext-before-Initialize, scorer/router registration) is sound. Adequate

06 /  Trading-logic assessment

The edge, the engines, and the discarded scorer

Two lanes carry the trading thesis: Trade-Signal (graded Weak) and Trade-Risk (graded Adequate). The signal lane is where the multi-strategy branch's incoherence concentrates; the risk lane is where the long-biased gold edge is respected but its drawdown controls leak.

The naive trigger and the re-enabled losers

The feat/multi-strategy branch layers a four-engine, regime-router, “orthogonal” confluence-scorer stack on top of the v18 long-biased gold EA — all gated off by default (InpEnableMultiStrategy=false), so it harms nothing in the shipped baseline today. But as a design it does not serve a real gold edge: the engines re-compose and re-activate the exact strategies the repo's own forensics killed — FVG Mitigation (PF 0.61), Pullback (−0.5R), BB Mean Reversion (−1.1R), SFP / Liquidity-Sweep (0% WR), short-side reversal logic. This is the single CRITICAL finding, and it is forward-looking by nature: the dormant code is double-gated off, but the dead-path defense lives in input flags, not inside the engines, and the entire point of the scaffold is to flip those engines on. The remediation mandate is unambiguous — no engine path may be enabled until it independently beats the v18 baseline with a stated kill-criterion (PF<1.0 or avg-R<0).

The scorer that gets computed and thrown away

The new CConfluenceScorer was supposed to discipline those engines. Instead it is computed and then discarded: the orchestrator's signal loop unconditionally re-runs the legacy comment-token evaluator, which overwrites setupQuality/qualityScore for every signal regardless of engine origin. An engine signal's tier — and its 0.6–1.5% risk — is decided by a hardcoded substring match on the comment string, not by the SMC context the engine actually evaluated. The orthogonal-axis redesign is dead weight in the live path. Worse, the two halves that do survive are both compromised: the scorer's headline “hard gate” rides a latched BOS spine that never resets — m_last_bos is set on a break and never cleared, so after the first structure break on gold the spine gate is permanently open — and the scorer's axes are not orthogonal: dealing-range, equilibrium, premium/discount and the SL anchor all derive from a single swing high/low pair, so one signal is counted as several and the point total is inflated toward the high-risk A/A+ band.

The synthesis

The multi-strategy thesis is incoherent against the measured edge: it re-introduces negative-expectancy flow, governs it with a scorer that is discarded before it can gate anything, and the one gate that runs is defeated by a latch. The repair order is to pick a single scorer (honor the engine tier or delete the duplicate), give the spine a freshness window and a closed-bar read, de-correlate the axes, and prove each engine against baseline before it is ever enabled.

Risk architecture: sound math, leaking ceilings

The risk and exit architecture is coherent and respects the repo's measured long-biased gold edge — fixed-fractional sizing with a correctly derived per-point value, the EC v3 controller and regime scaler independently bounded with floors, and the “trailing is sacred” Chandelier exit left intact per the empirical Core Truths. The gaps are at the portfolio level: the documented InpMaxTotalExposure cap is never enforced — only a position-count cap exists, so up to five concurrent correlated gold longs at 2.0% each can stack to ~10% aggregate at-risk against a named 5% ceiling that silently does nothing. The consecutive-loss de-risking scaler is fed the runner-leg PnL instead of total trade PnL, so a trade that banks TP0–TP2 partials but closes its runner red is recorded as a loss, corrupting the de-risk trigger. And two independent daily-loss halts use different day boundaries and P&L definitions, which can disagree around the rollover. None place a mathematically wrong single trade, but together they materially weaken drawdown control on exactly the correlated single-instrument book this EA runs.

07 /  Cross-cutting risks

Patterns that span subsystems

Three classes of defect recur across otherwise-unrelated lanes — they are the audit's most generalizable findings, because fixing one instance teaches the fix for the rest.

Forming-bar [0] reads where the convention is closed-bar [1]

This is the single most pervasive pattern. CMarketContext.Update() fires once, at the open of a new H1 bar — the moment shift [0] is a one-tick, near-empty candle. Yet SMC BOS/CHoCH/sweep detection, the trend / regime / macro / momentum classifiers, the cached dealing-range and swing points, the live CPullbackContinuationEngine ATR (and through it its stop distance), every ATR/SAR trailing plugin (invoked per-tick), and the file-signal runner's swing-high/low trailing all read the forming bar. The consequences range from mis-timed structure breaks that distort the scorer's spine, through intra-bar repaint of gating conditions, to wick-driven trailing stops that tighten on a spike that later retraces. The fix is uniform: read shift [1] (the closed bar), matching the discipline the candlestick entry plugins already follow. Note the cross-reviewer downgraded many of these — once-per-bar throttling makes them a stable per-bar accuracy bias rather than intra-bar flicker — but kept the live, P&L-relevant instances (PBC ATR, SMC BOS, file-signal trailing) at high or medium.

Persistence and multi-symbol isolation

The EA's CFileEntry genuinely supports and normalizes multi-symbol CSV signals (XAUUSD+ / DJ30 / NAS100 / BTCUSD), but the position-management and recovery layers were written as if single-symbol. The position coordinator manages off-chart file positions using the chart symbol _Symbol for ATR trailing, swing-stop levels and risk-dollar math — so a NAS100 file signal on a gold chart gets a stop computed from gold's ATR and gold's swing highs, a structurally meaningless SL. On restart, the persistence/recovery loaders match positions by magic number only, not symbol (unlike the live OnTick orphan path, which is symbol-aware), so the coordinator can adopt a foreign-symbol broker position and apply gold-derived math to it. Both are conditional on multi-symbol file use being enabled — irrelevant to a pure single-chart XAUUSD deployment — but a direct capital risk the moment that condition holds.

Data integrity and lost-signal edge cases

The end-to-end pipeline is well-engineered — event-driven saves, CRC32 over POD-only structs, robust netting-ticket correction. The integrity gaps are in edge cases: a file signal is marked executed before the orchestrator can reject it, so a transient reject (spread spike, momentary slippage) permanently loses a signal that a per-tick retry seconds later would have filled; and a 3-way file-signal position restored after a mid-trade restart silently loses its tp3 target (not in PersistedPosition), downgrading to a 2-way exit with no logged degradation. Neither corrupts data, but both silently change intended trading behavior.
