06Signal Validation Pipeline

All entry signals flow through these stages:

  1. Trend/Regime validation — D1/H4 trend, regime check (LONG only)
  2. Volume/Spread validation — breakout patterns only
  3. SMC Confluence scoring — order block, min 40/100 (LONG only)
  4. Pattern Confidence — ATR/ADX/MA analysis, min 40/100 (LONG only)
  5. Quality evaluation — point scoring → tier (all signals)
  6. Signal ranking — best quality score wins per bar
  7. Confirmation candle — 1-bar delay (trend patterns only)
Bypasses: SHORT signals skip the full validator (ATR minimum only). S3 / S6 mean-reversion execute immediately without confirmation candle.

08Glossary — Jargon Decoded

Plain-English definitions for the terms used in this document.

TermWhat it means
ATRAverage True Range — how much price typically moves per bar. Used to size stops and detect "big" candles.
RSIRelative Strength Index (0–100). Below 30 = oversold, above 70 = overbought.
ADXAverage Directional Index — how strong a trend is. Above 25 = strong trend.
EMA / MAExponential / Moving Average of price. EMA21 = average of last 21 bars.
D1 / H4 / H1 / M15Daily / 4-hour / 1-hour / 15-minute timeframe.
Long / ShortLong = buy expecting price up. Short = sell expecting price down.
SL / TPStop Loss (exit if wrong) / Take Profit (exit if right).
R / R-multipleRisk unit. +1R = made 1× what you risked. -1R = lost what you risked.
PFProfit Factor: gross wins ÷ gross losses. > 1 is profitable.
WRWin Rate: % of trades that closed profitable.
Order Block (OB)Where institutions placed large orders — visible as a strong candle that started a big move.
FVGFair Value Gap — a 3-candle pattern with a price "gap" between bars, often filled later.
Liquidity SweepPrice briefly pokes past a known level to trigger stops, then reverses.
Death CrossEMA50 crosses below EMA200 on Daily — classic long-term bear signal.
SessionTime blocks: Asia (overnight, quiet), London (active EU hours), NY (most volatile).
Confirmation candle1-bar delay before entry — wait for next candle to confirm the signal didn't fail immediately.
Mean reversion"Price goes too far → it'll come back." Opposite of trend-following.
Setup qualityPoint-based score (A+, A, B+) deciding how much to risk. A+ trades risk 1.5%, B+ risk 0.75%.

09Strategy Definitions — Active

Plain-English explanations of what each strategy actually does in the market.

1. Bullish Engulfing — "Buyers just overwhelmed sellers"
CEngulfingEntry · LONG · 287 trades · 44% WR · +$9,585
What you see A small red (down) candle, immediately followed by a large green (up) candle whose body is bigger than the previous red one's body — it "swallows" it.
Why it works Sellers were in control, then buyers showed up in such force that they erased the previous bar's selling in a single hour. That's often the first sign of a real reversal.
How the EA trades it Long-only (gold's upward bias). Waits one bar after the engulfing pattern (confirmation) before entering, to filter fakeouts.
Biggest dollar contributor in the entire system. ~36% of total profit.
2. Bullish Pin Bar — "Price tried to drop and got rejected"
CPinBarEntry · LONG · 247 trades · 42% WR · +$8,359
What you see A single candle with a tiny body near the top and a very long wick (tail) sticking down. Wick ≥ 2× body size.
Why it works Sellers pushed price way down during the hour — but buyers stepped in aggressively and bought it all back up. The long wick is the visual fingerprint of that rejection.
How the EA trades it Long only, with 1-bar confirmation. Longer wick relative to body = stronger signal.
3. Bullish MA Cross — "Trend just flipped from down to up"
CMACrossEntry · LONG · 58 trades · 52% WR · +$6,324
What you see The faster moving average (10-period) crosses upward through the slower one (21-period).
Why it works When short-term trend crosses above long-term trend, recent buying is stronger than recent selling — momentum has shifted.
How the EA trades it Long-only, blocked during NY session (late-day mean reversion frequently traps trend signals). Bearish MA Cross is hardcoded off.
Highest R per trade of any strategy at +0.360 R. Rare (~10/year) but high value.
4. Bearish Pin Bar — "Price tried to rally and got rejected"
CPinBarEntry · SHORT · 181 trades · 43% WR · +$2,344
What you see Mirror of the bullish pin bar — tiny body near the bottom, long upper wick.
How the EA trades it Short-only. NY session blocked (NY pin shorts lose -1.9R aggregate). Asia + London allowed. Executes immediately — no confirmation candle (gold's bias makes short windows brief).
5. Rubber Band Short (Death Cross) — "Stretched too far up in a downtrend"
CExpansionEngine · SHORT · 96 trades · 49% WR · +$782
What you see Daily Death Cross active (EMA50 < EMA200), and H1 price has just bounced more than 1.5× ATR above EMA21. Like a rubber band stretched too far.
Why it works Dead-cat bounces in bear regimes are sellable. When the corrective rally stretches too far from trend, mean-reversion kicks in.
How the EA trades it Requires D1 Death Cross + price stretched + ADX > 18 + A+ quality only. B+ versions lose -3.3R / 19 trades.
6. S3 Range Edge Fade — "Fade the breakout, it's fake"
CRangeEdgeFade · BOTH directions
What you see Price bouncing inside a horizontal range (30-bar Donchian box). Suddenly it pokes outside, then immediately snaps back inside.
Why it works False breakouts are very common — large traders push price past obvious levels to trigger stops, then reverse. This strategy fades those failures.
Trade management Anti-stall: 5 M15 bars no progress → cut 50%. 8 bars → close fully.
7. S6 Failed Break Reversal — "Stop hunt → snap back"
CFailedBreakReversal · LONG only
What you see Price spikes sharply past a known level (PDH/PDL, Weekly H/L, Asia H/L), then immediately snaps back the other way.
Why it works Textbook "stop hunt" — institutions push past obvious stop clusters to harvest liquidity, then the original move resumes.
How the EA trades it LONG only. Short side disabled (-8.9R / 6 years — gold's upward bias means failed breaks down are just normal pullbacks).
8. IC Breakout (Institutional Candle) — "A whale just showed up"
CExpansionEngine · BOTH directions
What you see A huge candle (body ≥ 1.8× ATR), then 2–5 quiet consolidation bars inside its range, then a breakout in the IC's direction.
Why it works A candle that large was likely caused by institutional activity. The consolidation is the market digesting; the breakout signals continued accumulation.
9. Range Box Entry — "Trade the box breakout"
CRangeBoxEntry · BOTH directions · low frequency
What you see Price consolidates in a clear box, then breaks out with conviction.
How the EA trades it Active but mostly replaced by S3 Range Edge Fade in practice — fading the false break tends to win more often on gold than the breakout play itself.
10. Displacement Entry — "Sweep + sharp directional move"
CDisplacementEntry · BOTH directions
What you see Price sweeps a liquidity level, then immediately prints a displacement candle (body ≥ 1.8× ATR) in the new direction.
Why it works SMC pattern. The sweep grabs stops; the displacement reveals where big money is going. The two events together are stronger than either alone.
11. Session Breakout — "Asian range breakout"
CSessionBreakoutEntry · BOTH directions
What you see During Asia (overnight), gold trades in a tight range. London open (~03:00 EST) breaks it decisively.
How the EA trades it Identifies Asia range high/low, waits for breakout, then waits for a retest of the breakout level before entering (filters fakeouts).
12. Volatility Breakout — "Channel breakout with momentum"
CVolatilityBreakoutEntry · BOTH directions
What you see Price breaks above the upper Donchian channel and outside the Keltner envelope, with ADX > 25.
Why it works Two different channel types must agree (reduces false signals). ADX filter ensures real trend strength, not just a random spike.
13. Crash Breakout — "Panic move during NY hours"
CCrashBreakoutEntry · BOTH directions · 13–17 GMT only
What you see Sharp ATR spike + RSI in an extreme zone, during 13:00–17:00 GMT (NY open through mid-session).
Why it works Gold's news-driven panic moves at NY open. This strategy is built to participate — but only during the hours when they actually happen.
14. False Breakout Fade — "Trade against the failed breakout"
CFalseBreakoutFadeEntry · BOTH directions
What you see Price breaks a level, fails to follow through, and reverses.
How the EA trades it Same concept as S3 but applied more broadly. Mostly superseded by S3 in practice.

10Why Strategies Were Killed

The disabled list is longer than the active list. Here's the story behind each kill:

💀 Bearish Engulfing
What it did Mirror of #1, but shorting on big red candles engulfing prior green.
Result: Lost -35.3R across 660 trades, 37% WR. Gold's long-term uptrend steamrolled every "bearish reversal" pattern. The single biggest improvement to the system came from turning this off.
💀 Bearish MA Cross
What it did Fast MA crosses below slow MA → sell signal.
Hardcoded off. Fights gold's secular uptrend. Even "perfect" crosses tend to resume up before the trade can profit.
💀 S6 Short
What it did Failed-break reversal going short.
-8.9R over 6 years. Failed breaks downside on gold are usually just normal pullbacks, not stop hunts. The structural asymmetry only works long.
💀 Pullback Continuation
What it did Buy the dip in an uptrend / sell the rally in a downtrend.
-0.5R / 38 trades. Even the multi-cycle re-entry variant failed. Gold's pullbacks don't have a clean enough structure for systematic entry.
💀 BB Mean Reversion
What it did Buy/sell at Bollinger Band extremes.
-1.1R / 10 trades. Never positive in any test period. Bollinger Bands on gold expand too aggressively to fade.
💀 FVG Mitigation
What it did Trade when price returned to fill a Fair Value Gap.
PF 0.61 — consistent loser. Forensics revealed FVGs on gold H1 act as momentum signals (price keeps going) not imbalance signals (price returns to fill). The entire premise was wrong for this market.
💀 SFP (Swing Failure)
What it did Trade when a swing high/low fails to hold.
0% win rate in 5.5 months. Dead on arrival.
💀 Silver Bullet
What it did ICT concept — trade specific 1-hour windows (e.g. 10–11 AM NY).
-2.1R over 6 years. The "magic hours" concept has no measurable edge on gold.
💀 London Close Reversal
27% WR, -$229 net. Pattern exists visually but isn't systematically tradeable.
💀 Compression Breakout · Panic Momentum · London BO · NY Continuation
Various PFs < 1 and 0% WRs. All either inconsistent or dead on arrival. Compression BO (PF 0.52), Panic Momentum (PF 0.47), London BO (0% WR), NY Continuation (0% WR).

12The Four Lessons

Meta-takeaways from every disabled strategy:

1. Gold's long-term uptrend punishes short-side trend strategies.
Killed Bearish Engulfing, Bearish MA Cross, S6 Short, Panic Momentum.
2. ICT/SMC concepts (FVG, SFP, Silver Bullet) don't have a measurable edge on gold H1.
They work in theory; they don't survive in backtests.
3. Pullback and mean-reversion strategies need extremely clean structure.
Gold has too much noise for systematic dip-buying or band-fading to work.
4. Trailing stops are sacred — never modify them.
Every attempt to "improve" exits (5 separate A/B tests in v17–v18) made the system worse. The optimal Chandelier 3.0× ATR setting was found empirically and is locked.
